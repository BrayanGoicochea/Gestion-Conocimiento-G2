The literature reveals a highly centralized and chemistry-driven research ﬁeld dominated by vector-control and pest-management studies, with limited mechanistic pharmacology in mammalian systems. Chemometric results demonstrate pronounced chemical polymorphism, separating monoterpene-rich and sesquiterpene-rich chemotypes. Monoterpene proﬁles, particularly those dominated by b-myrcene and aliphatic ketones, are mainly linked to insecticidal and repellent effects, whereas sesquiterpene-rich oils containing germacrene D, curzerenone, spathulenol, and a-bisabolol are more frequently associated with antimicrobial, antiparasitic, enzymeinhibitory, and antitumoral activities. Despite promising results, most studies remain preliminary. Pharmacological translation will require chemotype standardization, mechanistic validation, safety proﬁling, and advanced formulation strategies to enable reproducible and clinically relevant applications. Keywords Chemotype variability  Multivariate statistics  Principal component analysis  Pharmacological screening  Natural product bioactivity Introduction Medicinal and aromatic plants (MAPs), traditionally used for therapeutic purposes, are currently important raw materials for the production of natural compounds with a wide range of applications, including cosmetic, food, biomedical, and industrial products (Oliveira 2022). Among their derivatives, essential oils (EOs) stand out as complex mixtures of bioactive secondary metabolites, which have attracted considerable interest due to their effective therapeutic properties, supported by ethnopharmacological knowledge and traditional uses, as well as by their favorable chemical compositions(Fierascu et al. 2021; Pires et al. 2021; Dias et al. 2021; Ferreira et al. 2024). Numerous health-beneﬁcial biological properties associated with EOs have been reported, including their use in the prevention and management in the oxidative stress, as well as cardiovascular, infectious, respiratory, neoplastic, and inﬂammatory diseases (Fierascu et al. 2021; Pinto et al. 2021). However, these biological activities vary according to their speciﬁc chemical compositions, which may be inﬂuenced by environmental, genetic, and methodological factors. This variability highlights the pronounced chemical diversity of EOs and underscores the importance of characterizing their different chemotypes (de Souza et al. 2018; Burneo et al. 2021; Botelho et al. 2025). Currently, several plant groups have been investigated due to their chemical diversity, including species of the genus Siparuna. Belonging to the family Siparunaceae, this neotropical genus is distributed from Mexico to Paraguay and comprises aromatic trees or shrubs characterized by simple, opposite leaves, inconspicuous ﬂowers, and multiple small globose fruits with a distinctive pungent odor (Hall and Meirelles 2017). The genus includes approximately 60 species, of which about 20 occur in Brazil, except in the states of Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul, with the Amazon region harboring the highest diversity, accounting for 19 species (Brunassi et al. 2022; Peixoto 2026). Although they are neither ornamental nor fruitbearing plants, some Siparuna species exhibit significant economic, therapeutic, and phytochemical potential due to their ability to produce EOs and their extensive use in traditional medicine by different Indigenous and riverside communities across Central and South America (Brunassi and Lı´rio 2025). Leaves, fruits, and bark are commonly used by traditional healers in spiritual and religious rituals, as well as to treat conditions ranging from snake bites, wounds, fevers, and colds to gastrointestinal, infectious, respiratory, and rheumatic diseases(Burneo et al. 2021; Morocho et al. 2023; Brunassi and Lı´rio 2025). More recent studies have described the main chemical groups most frequently reported in EOs of the genus, namely monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes, present in both hydrocarbon and oxygenated forms. In the EO of Siparuna cymosa, the presence of abisabolol, p-cymen-9-ol, and spathulenol was reported as major constituents, exhibiting moderate cytotoxic activity against leukemic tumor cells in vitro (Oliveira et al. 2020). Spathulenol was also identiﬁed as the major compound in S. thecaphora, followed by 2-tridecanone, a-copaene, a-cadinol, and d-cadinene; in this species, the EO showed strong bacteriostatic activity (Vila et al. 2002). 123 5406 Phytochem Rev (2026) 25:5405–5446

Studies on S. schimpfﬁand S. aspera identiﬁed germacrene D, bicyclogermacrene, and a-pinene as the main constituents, whereas S. macrotepala presented dcadinene instead of a-pinene; the EOs of the latter two species exhibited relevant antioxidant and antimicrobial activities (Paco Fernando Noriega Rivera, Alexandra Guerrini and Tsamaraint 2014; Noriega et al. 2019). In the EO obtained from the leaves and fruits of S. muricata, high concentrations of a-pinene, limonene, b-acorenol, b-pinene, and camphene were reported, demonstrating moderate antioxidant activity and strong antimicrobial and anticholinesterase effects. Comparatively, S. guianensis also exhibited anticholinesterase activity, with an EO rich in shyobunone and its derivatives (45.5% of the total EO), reinforcing the neuroprotective potential of the genus (Martins et al. 2021; Brunassi and Lı´rio 2025). Among the Siparunaceae, the species Siparuna guianensis, popularly known as ‘‘negramina’’ and ‘‘capitiu´,’’ stands out for being frequently used as a model in phytochemical and pharmacological studies, mainly due to its chemical richness, reported biological properties, and wide geographic distribution, occurring across all Brazilian biomes (Martins et al. 2021; Brunassi and Lı´rio 2025). Several biological activities have already been investigated for this species, including antimicrobial (de Jesus et al. 2020), antiparasitic (Andrade et al. 2016), and anthelmintic activities (Carvalho et al. 2020), among others; however, the most frequently reported properties of its EO are its insecticidal and repellent potentials (Andrade et al. 2015; Ferreira et al. 2020; Montan˜o-Campaz et al. 2025). Despite its extensive traditional use and growing scientiﬁc interest, studies involving the essential oil of S. guianensis remain limited, scattered, and poorly integrated, particularly with respect to linking phytochemical variability to biological outcomes beyond in vitro models. In this context, the aim of this study was to integrate scientometric analysis, multivariate chemometric proﬁling, and critical literature review to systematically organize the existing knowledge on the chemical composition, antioxidant capacity, and biological activities of S. guianensis essential oil. By combining these complementary approaches, this study provides a structured framework to identify research trends, deﬁne chemotype patterns, and establish associations between chemical variability and biological activity. This integrative strategy is expected to support the rational interpretation of fragmented data, guide future experimental design, and facilitate the development of more reproducible and translational studies, thereby contributing to advancing the scientiﬁc understanding and pharmacological potential of this species.. Methodology Bibliometric procedures A bibliometric analysis was performed following procedures previously established by our research group (Maia et al. 2023; Conceic¸a˜o et al. 2023; Silva et al. 2025). The Web of Science Core Collection (WoS-CC) database was used as the primary data source to retrieve publications related to Siparuna guianensis essential oil and its biological and antioxidant properties. The literature search was performed on 06 October 2026, using a predeﬁned search strategy ([Siparuna OR ‘‘Siparuna guianensis’’ OR ‘‘S. guianensis’’ OR Negramina] AND [‘‘Essential oil’’ OR ‘‘Volatile oil’’]). Eligible documents included only original research articles addressing the chemical composition and biological activities of S. guianensis essential oil, with no language restrictions applied. Review articles, conference papers, editorials, letters, proceedings, ethnobotanical studies, articles not freely available, and publications lacking biological evaluation were excluded. After establishing the inclusion criteria, two independent researchers screened titles and abstracts. Disagreements regarding article eligibility were resolved by consultation with a third senior researcher. Bibliometric indicators were extracted from WoSCC, including article title, publication year, authors, number of citations, journals, countries, institutions, and author keywords. Network visualization and quantitative mapping were performed using VOSviewer software (version 1.6.16). In the generated networks, nodes represent authors, institutions, or keywords, while links indicate co-authorship, interinstitutional collaboration, or keyword co-occurrence; node size is proportional to publication or citation frequency, and link strength reﬂects the intensity of collaboration or thematic association. Multivariate analysis methodology (PCA and HCA) The chemical composition data of Siparuna guianensis (Aubl.), compiled from different studies reported in the literature, were organized into a data matrix in which rows represented the samples and columns corresponded to the identiﬁed chemical constituents, expressed as relative percentage (%). Only compounds reported in the selected studies were included in the multivariate analysis. Prior to the statistical analyses, the data were subjected to autoscaling standardization (mean-centered and variance-scaled to unit variance) in order to eliminate the inﬂuence of differences in variable magnitude and to ensure an equivalent contribution of all chemical constituents. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was applied as an exploratory method to reduce data dimensionality and to identify patterns of similarity and variability among samples, considering variations associated with different years of collection and reported chemical proﬁles. The principal components were selected based on eigenvalues greater than 1 (Kaiser criterion) and cumulative explained variance, with emphasis on those components accounting for the largest proportion of the total variance. The PCA results were interpreted using score plots, which allowed visualization of sample distribution and grouping, and loading plots, which were used to identify the chemical compounds most responsible for sample discrimination. Hierarchical Cluster Analysis (HCA) was performed as a complementary technique to PCA in order to assess the similarity among samples and to conﬁrm the clustering patterns observed. The full texts of all included articles were carefully examined, focusing on chemical composition, major constituents, and biological activity. Results and discussion Bibliometric results The search strategy applied in WoS-CC retrieved 74 documents related to Siparuna guianensis. After applying the predeﬁned inclusion criteria, 25 articles were selected for detailed qualitative and quantitative evaluation. This marked reduction indicates that, although S. guianensis has been extensively investigated from botanical and phytochemical perspectives, only approximately 34% of the available literature establishes a direct association between volatile composition and biological function. This ﬁnding reveals a fragmented research landscape characterized by abundant chemical proﬁling but comparatively limited bioprospecting of biological activities. The earliest and most cited study was published in 2013 and investigated the chemical composition and antioxidant properties of S. guianensis essential oil; however, no antioxidant activity was detected (Andrade et al. 2013). In contrast, the most recent publication (2025) employed an integrated approach combining phytochemical characterization, in silico molecular docking, and in vitro bioassays to demonstrate larvicidal activity, highlighting acetylcholinesterase inhibition as a potential mechanism of action (Montan˜o-Campaz et al. 2025) (Table 1). The analysis of the selected articles indicates that research on S. guianensis essential oil is predominantly centered on insecticidal and vector-control applications, particularly against Aedes aegypti and agricultural pests, which also account for the most highly cited publications. In contrast, studies approaching antimicrobial, antioxidant, antiparasitic, or antiviral activities generally exhibit lower citation impact and often report only moderate efﬁcacy which highlights the peripheral role of S. guianensis in these research domains. Phytochemical data reveal pronounced intraspeciﬁc variability, with b-myrcene, a-bisabolol, spathulenol, curzerenone, germacrene D, c-elemene, and shyobunone derivatives emerging as recurrent major constituents (a comprehensive phytochemical characterization is addressed in Sect. ‘‘Chemical compositions Siparuna guianensis’’). Biological activity appears to be strongly chemotype-dependent, as evidenced by studies demonstrating superior acaricidal and anthelmintic effects in a-bisabolol-rich oils and consistent insecticidal performance in b-myrcene, dominant proﬁles. More recent investigations increasingly integrate molecular docking, enzyme inhibition assays, and membrane permeability studies, providing more sophisticated biological assays. Antioxidant potential Due to the high chemical variability of EOs, it is common to evaluate their speciﬁc antioxidant activities, which, even within the same species, may vary depending on their major chemical constituents; moreover, the different methods used to assess antioxidant activity in biological systems are relatively fast, simple, and inexpensive, which justiﬁes their application in phytochemical studies (Huang et al. 2025). Among the selected studies, all were conducted in Brazil, and the most commonly used method was the quantitative 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging assay, one of the most widely applied free radical scavenging assays according to the literature; in addition, the second most frequently used method was the lipid peroxidation assay based on the b-carotene/linoleic acid cooxidation method (Mendonc¸a et al. 2022). biological activity potential Repellent and insecticidal activity The essential oil of Siparuna guianensis has attracted considerable pharmacological interest; however, an important aspect to be highlighted in this review is its potent activity against insect vectors and agricultural pests (Ferreira et al. 2019). Among the biological properties most consistently reported, insecticidal and repellent activities are the most frequently investigated, reﬂecting the growing body of research focused on this ﬁeld. This increasing interest is supported by experimental evidence demonstrating the effectiveness of this species against agricultural pests as well as mosquitoes of medical importance, underscoring its potential for further exploration in vector control and pest management strategies (Aguiar et al. 2015; Ferreira et al. 2017; Ferreira et al. 2019; Montan˜oCampaz et al. 2025). An important study highlighted in this section investigated the potential of nanoemulsiﬁcation of S. guianensis essential oil against mosquito vectors of dengue, Zika, and chikungunya viruses. The results showed that this formulation strategy substantially enhanced larvicidal activity compared to the nonformulated oil, demonstrating that appropriate delivery systems can signiﬁcantly improve bioefﬁcacy (Ferreira et al. 2019). In line with these ﬁndings, encapsulation of S. guianensis essential oil in chitosan nanoparticles prolonged its residual mosquitocidal activity against Aedes aegypti and maintained larvicidal effectiveness over time, indicating that nanostructured carriers can modulate release kinetics and protect volatile phytochemicals from rapid degradation (Ferreira et al. 2020). Additional investigations have also demonstrated insecticidal and repellent activity of the oil against Culex quinquefasciatus, another urban mosquito vector of epidemiological relevance, reinforcing the broad applicability of this species in mosquito control strategies (Aguiar et al. 2015). In addition to its notable activity against mosquito larvae, the insecticidal spectrum of S. guianensis essential oil extends to insects of major agricultural and economic importance. A study evaluating this oil demonstrated activity against wax moth species (Galleria mellonella and Achroia grisella), pests commonly associated with beekeeping, while also showing selective toxicity with reduced adverse effects on honey bees (Apis mellifera), a crucial nontarget pollinator (Ferreira et al. 2017). This broader spectrum of activity highlights the versatility of S. guianensis essential oil within integrated pest management frameworks. Another reported activity involves beetles such as Tribolium castaneum, an important pest of stored grains. The study indicated that essential oils from Neotropical plant species, including S. guianensis, may be applicable for the control of this type of agricultural pest (Pino-Benitez et al. 2024), highlighting the relevance of research exploring the potential of essential oils as botanical insecticides. In addition to investigating direct toxicity, some studies have explored the repellent capacity of the essential oil associated with the reduction of hostseeking and oviposition behavior in insects, particularly mosquitoes. This deterrent effect is associated with the volatile terpenoid proﬁle characteristic of the genus, rich in monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes that interfere with the olfactory and neurobehavioral pathways of insects (Aguiar et al. 2015; Montan˜oCampaz et al. 2025). Beyond direct toxicity, the repellent properties of S. guianensis essential oil have been implicated in reducing insect host-seeking and oviposition behaviors, particularly in mosquito species. Experimental studies evaluating both insecticidal and behavioral endpoints support a deterrent effect associated with the volatile terpenoid proﬁle characteristic of the genus, which is rich in monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes known to interfere with insect olfactory and neurobehavioral pathways (Aguiar et al. 2015; Montan˜o-Campaz et al. 2025). Importantly, the integration of formulation technologies such as nanoemulsions, biopolymeric nanoparticles, and starch-based microparticles has been shown to enhance the insecticidal performance of S. guianensis essential oil while potentially improving its environmental compatibility. Encapsulation approaches using biodegradable matrices can increase stability, modulate release, and prolong biological activity, helping to overcome common limitations of essential oils, such as volatility and rapid degradation under ﬁeld conditions. Overall, the available experimental evidence indicates that the essential oil of S. guianensis represents a promising bioactive resource with insecticidal and repellent potential against urban vectors, agricultural 123 5438 Phytochem Rev (2026) 25:5405–5446

pests, and storage insects, supporting further research that integrates phytochemical characterization, advanced delivery systems, ecological safety, and resistance-management perspectives toward the development of sustainable pest and vector control strategies. Antimicrobial activity Essential oils are volatile compounds that are primarily synthesized as secondary metabolites of glucose metabolism in plants and are mostly obtained through processes such as hydrodistillation or steam distillation from various plant structures. The essential oil of S. guianensis contains sesquiterpenes, oxygenated sesquiterpenes, and monoterpene hydrocarbons, which predominantly exhibit hydrophobic characteristics (Santana de Oliveira et al. 2020). Gram-negative bacteria possess an outer membrane rich in lipopolysaccharides (LPS), which confers a hydrophilic surface; therefore, hydrophobic compounds are generally unable to cross these intact membranes (Cox et al. 2001). A study conducted by Andrade et al. (Andrade et al. 2015) demonstrated that S. guianensis essential oil exhibited inhibitory effects against Gram-positive bacteria such as Listeria monocytogenes and Staphylococcus aureus but showed no inhibitory activity against Gram-negative bacteria. The compound apinene showed the highest biological activity against L. monocytogenes. In addition, S. guianensis essential oil demonstrated strong antifungal activity, with lower inhibitory concentrations against Candida albicans, Cryptococcus neoformans, Trichophyton rubrum, and Aspergillus fumigatus. Santana de Oliveira et al., (2020) investigated the essential oil of S. guianensis and identiﬁed trans-belemenone and atractylone as the most abundant compounds in the sample. Molecular docking studies revealed positive interactions with target proteins, such as Candida albicans N-myristoyltransferase and Escherichia coli enoyl reductase. The predominance of hydrophobic interactions suggests that the ligand exhibits good complementarity with the active sites of the enzymes studied, which may contribute to increased complex stability and potential biological activity. In this context, the low RMSD values, combined with favorable energetic contributions obtained by the MM/GBSA method, reinforce the stability of the formed complexes and suggest good potential of the ligand as an inhibitor of the evaluated target proteins. The essential oil of S. guianensis also demonstrated inhibitory activity against Rhizoctonia solani, as well as inhibition of mycelial growth of Sclerotium rolfsii. In this study, the major components identiﬁed were the monoterpene b-myrcene and 2-undecanone (Arruda et al. 2019). De Jesus et al. (de Jesus et al. 2020) conducted a study to evaluate the use of essential oils as a therapeutic alternative for foodborne diseases. Compared to other oils analyzed, S. guianensis did not demonstrate antimicrobial activity against E. coli strains; however, it exhibited activity against Staphylococcus spp., including a multidrug-resistant strain, although this was not the most pronounced result of the study. In a study by Melo et al. (Melo et al. 2017), the most abundant compounds identiﬁed were the acyclic monoterpene b-myrcene and the sesquiterpenes germacrene D and (E, E)-farnesol. The essential oil showed antibacterial efﬁcacy against Streptococcus mutans strains. The same study also evaluated anticariogenic activity, which was evidenced by activity against S. sobrinus and S. mutans, in addition to antimycobacterial activity against Mycobacterium tuberculosis, M. kansasii, and M. avium. These effects were directly associated with the presence of viridiﬂorol compounds in the essential oil (Melo et al. 2017). Moura et al. (2020) investigated the essential oil of S. guianensis, whose composition was mainly characterized by b-myrcene, germacrene D, and espicurzerenone. Antimicrobial activity was observed against E. coli, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Streptococcus pyogenes, and Staphylococcus aureus. Even at low concentrations, inhibition of bacterial growth was observed, while higher concentrations completely inhibited growth. Additionally, the essential oil increased cellular potassium efﬂux. Nucleotide leakage was also observed following exposure to the essential oil, and the authors demonstrated that none of the concentrations tested caused toxicity to human monocytic cells (TPH1). The results obtained in this study indicate the potential efﬁcacy of the essential oil of Siparuna guianensis, particularly when compared with data reported for essential oils from other species or genera (da Costa et al. 2024; Ferreira et al. 2025). When compared to a reference antibiotic, the essential 123 Phytochem Rev (2026) 25:5405–5446 5439

oil exhibited a greater ability to alter bacterial membrane permeability, as evidenced by increased K? efﬂux. Molecular docking analyses suggest that compounds present in the essential oil, particularly germacrene B, exhibit high afﬁnity for active regions of bacterial DNA and RNA polymerases, potentially inducing conformational changes capable of inhibiting replication and transcription processes (Robinson and Oijen 2013). The bacteriostatic or bactericidal effect of S. guianensis essential oil may be explained by the inhibition of microbial growth due to damage to membrane integrity caused by lipophilic components present in the oil, leading to loss of cellular pH homeostasis and inorganic ions (Melo et al. 2017; Santana de Oliveira et al. 2020). Furthermore, increased potassium ion efﬂux and nucleotide leakage suggest that the essential oil compromises the integrity of the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane, allowing the release of essential intracellular components (Roosild et al. 2010; Bajpai et al. 2013). Additionally, it has been demonstrated that the phytochemical composition of S. guianensis essential oil is dependent on seasonality and collection site, resulting in variation of major and minor compounds. Overall, the ﬁndings indicate that Gram-positive bacteria are more sensitive to the effects of the essential oil, likely due to the volatile compounds present in its composition. Antiparasitic activity Few studies have demonstrated the activity of S. guianensis against intracellular protozoa. In an in vitro analysis, the ethanolic extract, ethyl acetate fraction, aqueous fraction, volatile oil, and isolated a-bisabolol from S. guianensis Aubl. showed activity against the RH2F1 strain of Toxoplasma gondii in ﬁbroblasts (Souza et al. 2021). After parasite infection, the fractions exhibited partial modulation of intracellular proliferation. When used as a pretreatment, the ethanolic extract and the ethyl acetate fraction directly inhibited the parasite (Souza et al. 2021). The essential oils obtained from the leaves of different chemotypes of S. guianensis, rich in abisabolol and germacrene D, demonstrated acaricidal activity against Rhipicephalus microplus. In larval proliferation assays, samples containing a-bisabolol exhibited a 100% mortality rate, although all essential oils caused high mortality in R. microplus. In addition, a-bisabolol–rich samples showed a reduction in both the number and viability of eggs (Diniz et al. 2022). Anthelmintic properties were evaluated for the ethanolic extract, essential oil, aqueous and ethyl acetate fractions of S. guianensis, as well as for the compound a-bisabolol, against eggs of Strongyloides venezuelensis. In larval motility and egg hatching assays, all samples exhibited ovicidal activity comparable to albendazole, in addition to inducing morphological alterations in the eggs (Carvalho et al. 2020). In a study evaluating sixteen essential oils from S. guianensis, activity was observed against the promastigote forms of Leishmania (L.) amazonensis (Andrade et al. 2016). The essential oil from S. guianensis leaves, composed of b-myrcene, germacrene D, and bicyclogermacrene, was the most effective in inhibiting protozoan growth; however, it exhibited higher cytotoxicity toward L6 and L cells compared with the other samples (Andrade et al. 2016). Overall, extracts, essential oils, and fractions of S. guianensis display inhibitory activity against parasites such as ticks, helminths, and protozoa, indicating potential for the treatment of related diseases. Antitumoral activity Traditionally known in the Amazon region as ‘‘capitiu´,’’ the species S. guianensis exhibits high variability in the chemotypes of its essential oils. Recent chemical studies have identiﬁed curzerenone (16.4%), drimenol (13.7%), and spathulenol (12.4%) as major constituents of the essential oil of this plant collected in Brazil. Curzerenone has been associated in bioactivity studies with potential cytotoxic effects in tumor cell models, including the ability to induce apoptosis in cancer cell lines, suggesting a possible antitumor role for its derivatives. Furthermore, spathulenol is frequently linked to immune system modulation and demonstrates antiproliferative properties in different biological systems, reinforcing the pharmacological interest in oxygenated sesquiterpenes present in the essential oil of S. guianensis. According to Barbosa et al. (2023), this proﬁle had not been previously described in the literature, strengthening the inﬂuence of edaphoclimatic, seasonal, and geographic factors on the synthesis of secondary metabolites. This ﬁnding contributes to expanding the phytochemical knowledge of the species and highlights the importance of 123 5440 Phytochem Rev (2026) 25:5405–5446

regional studies with Amazonian medicinal species (Martins et al. 2021). Barbosa et al. (2023) conducted a study that integrates chemical analyses (GFID and GC-MS), in vitro antioxidant assays (DPPH and b-carotene/ linoleic acid) and in vivo evaluation based on the experimental oncology model with the Ehrlich ascites carcinoma (EAC) model to test the efﬁcacy of S. guianensis essential oil. In this context, in vivo tests showed that after administration of 100 mg/kg of the oil, it not only reduced the volume of ascitic ﬂuid but also preserved the viability of hepatocytes, suggesting low systemic toxicity compared to conventional chemotherapeutic agents. Decreases in malondialdehyde (MDA) levels and increased superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity in liver tissues indicated a protective effect against tumor-induced oxidative stress. These results are consistent with the literature that associates the control of oxidative stress with the inhibition of tumor progression (Marnett 1999). The antitumor effect of S. guianensis essential oil can be deﬁned by its complex composition of sesquiterpenes, which has a synergistic effect that inhibits and protects the body against oxidative damage. In silico analyses In silico studies on S. guianensis have provided signiﬁcant insights into the potential molecular mechanisms underlying experimentally observed biological activities. These computational approaches have been primarily applied to establish correlations between the major sesquiterpenes present in the essential oil and relevant biological targets, employing methodologies such as homology modeling, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and binding energy calculations. Such strategies enable the rationalization of bioactivity at the molecular level and support the identiﬁcation of key ligand–target interactions (Toledo et al. 2019; Santana de Oliveira et al. 2020; Martins et al. 2021; Costa et al. 2022; Montan˜oCampaz et al. 2025). Research by Toledo et al. (2019) regarding insecticides and selectivity offered the use of computational modeling speciﬁcally with b-myrcene and 2-undecanone, hypothesizing that the main constituents of the essential oil of S. guianensis act as TRP (Transient Receptor Potential) channels of Myzus persicae. Therefore, homology modeling (Swiss-Model) integrated with molecular docking (Autodock 4) was employed in the study. The main results highlight that both compounds bound to the TRP channel, speciﬁcally the lipid activation site where 2-undecanone formed an immunity bond with Ser450 and hydrophobic van der Waals interactions with residues Y470, L451, M467, D446, S448, and T449, showing a modulating ligand proﬁle. b-Myrcene exhibited exclusively hydrophobic interactions with alkyl/p-alkyl type contacts with Y470, L451, M467, high compatibility with the lipid environment of the channel, which is consistent with the nonpolar nature of the compound (Adams 2007). Finally, when evaluating selectivity via docking, it was observed that no stable interaction was noted between the compounds and the TRP channel of the predictions. Further investigations using in silico studies were employed to support the larvicidal mechanism of action of Siparuna essential oils against Aedes aegypti, aiming to verify which essential mosquito proteins could be molecularly affected by the sesquiterpenes celemene and germacrene D (Montan˜o-Campaz et al. 2025). Thus, homology modeling of AChE was performed in the Swiss-Model, where high structural identity and model validation were veriﬁed with a Ramachandran of 93% of residues in favorable regions and a QMEAN factor of - 1.04, demonstrating that the enzyme structure is robust for docking. The data suggest that sesquiterpenes do not act as classic covalent inhibitors and likely exert non-competitive or mixed inhibition, in addition to interfering with the enzyme’s conformational dynamics, leading to acetylcholine accumulation and, conversely, neurotransmission dysregulation, resulting in larval paralysis and death. The study demonstrated that the ligands exhibited van der Waals interactions with important amino acid residues, with germacrene D showing an alkylhydrophobic interaction afﬁnity with Phe587 and celemene showing a p-alkyl interaction afﬁnity with Phe587 and Phe591 (Ramachandran and Sasisekharan 1968; Trott and Olson 2010; Waterhouse et al. 2018). Within the scope of prospecting for compounds with antimicrobial potential, an important study in this section focuses its analyses on molecular docking and molecular dynamics studies, in relation to metabolites such as atractylone, germacrene D and c-elemene, which showed signiﬁcant afﬁnity for essential molecular targets, including insect acetylcholinesterase 123 Phytochem Rev (2026) 25:5405–5446 5441

(AChE) and key enzymes involved in microbial metabolism (Andrade et al. 2013; Nelita et al. 2015). Molecular docking analyses revealed the predominance of stable hydrophobic interactions at the active site of the target proteins, as well as the punctual occurrence of hydrogen bonds with conserved catalytic residues. These results allowed the ranking of the compounds and the selection of the most promising complexes for the molecular dynamics stage. MD simulations demonstrated that the most active molecules form stable complexes with the target protein, exhibiting convergent RMSD and good ligand ﬁt, while RMSF showed reduced ﬂexibility of the active site residues and maintenance of the main intermolecular interactions over time. Free energy calculations by MM-PBSA conﬁrmed favorable binding afﬁnities, with a predominant contribution from hydrophobic interactions, corroborating the proposed antimicrobial mechanism. These interaction patterns suggest plausible mechanisms of enzymatic inhibition, corroborating the effects observed in in vitro and in vivo biological assays previously described in the literature (Melo et al. 2017; Santana de Oliveira et al. 2020). In the neurobiological ﬁeld, Martins et al. (Martins et al. 2021) conducted computational screenings targeting acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibition, which indicated that oxygenated sesquiterpenes, especially shyobunone and iso-shyobunone, showed satisfactory steric complementarity with the binding site and peripheral regions of the enzyme, with the latter being the strongest ligand as it presented the best binding energy (-8.0 kcal/mol). Docking of the two binding sites revealed two preferential pockets in the AChE catalytic channel. The innermost region, A, involves residues Trp108, Tyr146, Gly143-148, Try155, Phe315, Tyr355, and His494. This region proved to be the closest, but not coincident with, the classic catalytic triad (Ser-His-Glu). The outermost region, site B, peripheral to the enzyme, involves residues such as Tyr94, Trp304, Phe313-315, Tyr355, and Tyr359, which is associated with allosteric modulation of AChE. These ﬁndings support the hypothesis of a possible non-competitive inhibition mechanism, previously suggested by kinetic and spectroscopic analyses, and also showed dominant hydrophobic interactions and some stabilizing hydrogen bonds (Valentini et al. 2010; Andrade et al. 2015). Conclusions This integrated scientometric, chemometric, and pharmacological analysis demonstrates that Siparuna guianensis essential oil is a chemically polymorphic natural product whose biological performance is strongly chemotype-dependent. Current evidence supports consistent bioactivity in insecticidal, antiparasitic, antimicrobial, enzyme-inhibitory, and antitumoral models, yet the ﬁeld remains largely driven by chemical proﬁling and preliminary screening assays. Mechanistic pharmacology, dose, response characterization, and translational validation are still limited. Future research should prioritize chemotype standardization linked to botanical origin, plant organ, and extraction parameters to improve reproducibility across studies. Mechanism-oriented investigations using molecular, cellular, and systems pharmacology approaches are needed to conﬁrm proposed targets such as acetylcholinesterase, membrane integrity pathways, and replication enzymes. Comparative testing of isolated major constituents versus wholeoil matrices should be expanded to clarify synergistic and antagonistic interactions. In vivo pharmacokinetics, toxicology, and selectivity studies in mammalian models represent a critical gap that must be addressed before therapeutic consideration. Advanced delivery systems, including nanoformulations and encapsulation platforms, show promise for enhancing stability, bioavailability, and safety and should be systematically optimized. Finally, multicenter collaborative studies integrating metabolomics, chemometrics, and pharmacodynamics are recommended to establish predictive chemotype-activity models and accelerate the rational pharmacological development of S. guianensis essential oil.
