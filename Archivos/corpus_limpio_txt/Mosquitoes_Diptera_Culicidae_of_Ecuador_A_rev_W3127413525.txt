Abstract 34 Ecuador is one of the countries with the highest biodiversity in the world, this can also be seen in 35 the Culicidae family. However, there are a limited number of studies on this group. This work 36 provides a baseline reference for this insect group and information about numerous potential and 37 vector disease species. Species names and records were extracted from the National Mosquito 38 Reference Collection at INSPI-Quito, published literature and web databases. The specimens at 39 the INSPI collection were identified using morphological keys and, in a few cases, using 40 molecular markers in the genus Anopheles. An updated list includes the subfamilies Culicinae 41 and Anophelinae, eight tribes, 22 genera and 200 species. We present 18 species cataloged as 42 new records for Ecuador represented in two subfamilies, 6 tribes and 9 genera. Taxonomic notes, 43 geographical distribution and medical importance data for the species involved are provided. The 44 updated list of Mosquitoes (Diptera: Culicidae) is a guide for researchers and health personnel 45 when studying biodiversity, fauna, insect vectors and strategies to prevent the spread of vector 46 diseases. 47

Introduction 62 Ecuador is considered one of the most biodiverse countries in the world due to rich and unique 63 habitats. Arthropods dominate rainforest ecosystems in terms of species numbers and Ecuador is 64 among the countries recognized as one of the centers of speciation, because of its complex 65 geological history and climate (1). 66 Ecuador is located on the northwest coast of South America, and its territory includes the 67 continental mainland and the Galápagos Islands in the Pacific Ocean. The mainland of Ecuador 68 is divided geographically into the coastal lowlands on the west of the Andes Mountains, the 69 Andes or highlands, with valleys and mountains with snowed peaks of up to 6300 m, and 70 lowlands on the east of the Andes that are part of the Amazon basin. As a consequence of this 71 geographical diversity Ecuador has 26 Holdridge ecological life zones, one of the highest in the 72 Neotropics (2, 3). These characteristics make Ecuador one of the most biodiverse countries in the 73 world. This biological richness is apparent also in the Culicidae family. However, in Ecuador, 74 this group has been overlooked for the last 50 years. Just recently, records of new species have 75 been reported for the country (4-6). 76 There are around 3570 species of the family Culicidae (Diptera) in the world, distributed in 112 77 genera and 145 subgenera, and arranged in two subfamilies, Anophelinae and Culicinae. 78 Anophelinae has three genera and Culicinae 109 genera (7). The first descriptions of mosquitoes 79 from Ecuador were done by Alexander von Humbolt in 1819 (8). The study of the mosquito 80 fauna of Ecuador then continued by Francisco Campos who contributed several publications on 81 mosquitoes (9-12). Campos collected mosquitoes mainly around the city of Guayaquil and 82 coastal areas. Those specimens were sent to Harrison G. Dyar, who described several species 83 from Ecuador around 1918-1925 (13) and several species were named after Campos. 84 Levi-Castillo has been, so far, one of the most significant contributors to the knowledge of 85 mosquitos of Ecuador. Among his most important publications are the Atlas de los Anofelinos 86 Sudamericanos (14) and the first Culicidae checklist of the country (15). In 1962, Levi-Castillo 87 abruptly quit entomology, which resulted in almost no publications dealing with the mosquito 88 fauna from Ecuador since then. In the next decade, 28 species of Culicidae for Ecuador were 89 reported (8), 19 of which were described by Levi-Castillo. The latter author reported an extended 90 list of mosquitoes collected mainly on the coastal areas and the highlands (15). Heinemann and 91 Belkin (16) reported detailed data for 80 mosquito species from Ecuador. The last list reported 92 for Ecuador mentions 179 species (4). 93 This work provides a baseline reference for this insect group and information about numerous 94 potential and vector disease species. 95 Materials and Methods 96 Species names and records were extracted from the National Mosquito Reference Collection 97 (CIREV) at INSPI-Quito (Instituto Nacional de Investigación en Salud Publica), here cited as 98 INSPI collection, published literature and web databases (17-19). Aedes taeniorhynchus (Wiedemann, 1821) is vector of Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus 219 (VEEV) (44, 45). This species is considered a potential threat for the Galapagos Islands fauna as 220 a competent vector of West Nile virus (WNV) (46). 221 There are eleven species of Haemagogus mosquitoes reported for Ecuador. Haemagogus 222 leucocelaenus (Dyar & Shannon, 1924) is a vector of yellow fever virus (47, 48). Haemagogus 223 anastasionis Dyar, 1921 has been reported for Ecuador and it is a potential vector of yellow 224 fever and Mayaro virus (5). 225 Thirteen species of the genus Psorophora have been reported for Ecuador. Psorophora ferox 226 (Humboldt, 1819) has been reported as carrier of Ilheus virus (ILHV) in Central, South America 227 and Trinidad and Tobago. In Ecuador the virus isolate was obtained from a human, however, this 228 report needs to be confirmed due to cross-reactivity in serologic assays to other flaviviruses (49). 229 The genus Culex has 61 species in Ecuador, representing 8% of the world diversity. There are 14 230 species (19%) exclusively in the western lowlands of Ecuador, while 31 species (43%), of the 231 species are in the eastern lowlands, 13 species (18%) distributed on both sides of the Andean 232 mountains, and three species (4%) are in the highlands. 233 Culex (Culex) quinquefasciatus Say, 1823. This species is widely distributed on the west and east 234 of the Andean mountains of Ecuador, and has been introduced into the Galapagos Islands (15, 235 50). It is the most important species for transmitting filariasis and Oropouche virus (51), Culex 236 quinquefasciatus is an established species in the Galapagos Islands and it has been reported as 237 vector of avian malaria and avian pox virus. The genus Mansonia Blanchard, 1901 is represented by three species reported in Ecuador. 254 Mansonia titillans (Walker, 1948) is known to carry the virus of Venezuelan equine encephalitis 255 (55) and it may also transport eggs of Dermatobia hominis (60-63). 256 The genus Johnbelkinia Zavortink, 1979 is represented by two species. Johnbelkinia ulopus has 257 been mentioned as a potential vector of arbovirus (64, 65) and is known to carry eggs of 258 Dermatobia hominis in Colombia (66). 259 There are three species of the genus Limatus Theobald, 1901. Limatus durhami and Li. 260 flavisetosus have been reported carrying viruses (63). 261 There are 14 species of Sabethes reported for Ecuador. Sabethes chloropterus has been reported 262 as vector of yellow fever (67) and other arboviruses like St. Louis encephalitis and Ilhéus (60, 263 63). Sabethes amazonicus has been indicated as a potential vector of yellow fever virus (5). 264 There are 14 species of the genus Wyeomyia Theobald, 1901 from Ecuador. There are reports of 265 virus presence in species of this genus, however, it is not known their medical importance (60, 266 63). 267 Discussion 268 The updated mosquito species present in Ecuador, along with the specimens deposited in a 269 National Reference Collection, will help to understand the diversity and the dynamics of several 270 diseases transmitted by species of this group. Presently, the National Mosquito Reference 271 Collection (CIREV) at INSPI - Quito holds 98 species with detailed collection data, out of the 272 200 reported for Ecuador. The list presented, most likely, is a partial inventory of the Ecuadorian 273 mosquito fauna, considering that the country has one of the highest biodiversity in the region 274 (68) and many ecosystems have not been fully surveyed. Additionally, the updated record of 275 mosquito species in continental Ecuador is also relevant for the surveillance of invasive species 276 in fragile ecosystems like the Galapagos Islands. Extensive collections are further needed and 277 taxonomic and phylogenetic tools should be applied to establish the distribution, identity and 278 disease incrimination of species complexes. 279 The number of mosquito species for Ecuador is significant considering the country size, for 280 instance Brazil has 447 species of mosquitoes, while Mexico has about 225, Colombia 324 and 281 Peru 129 species (27, 69). Foley et al. reported only 118 species, 23 endemic species and 34 282 types for Ecuador (69). The most recent list of Culicidae of Ecuador (4) reports 179 species for 283 Ecuador. The number of mosquito species reported in this revision represents 6% of the 284 Culicidae species. Out of the 200 species, 13 (6.5%) have Ecuador as the type locality. Mosquito 285 taxonomy studies are still scarce in Ecuador and there are not published morphological 286 identification keys available for local mosquito fauna. The taxonomic status of several species 287 presents in Ecuador, in particular of the genus Anopheles that has several species complexes, 288 needs to be revised along with their vector disease status.
